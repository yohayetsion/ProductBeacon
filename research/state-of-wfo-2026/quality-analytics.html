How to read this report

The report grades five markets. Each chapter grades up to eight vendors (the cap), grouped in three tiers by market weight:

  • Gravity: the largest vendors. Their installed base pulls the market.
  • Attention: the credible mid-sized contenders worth tracking.
  • Wildcard: the newest or least-documented entrant graded. The tier measures the depth of the public record, not the company's health; the report asserts no vendor's viability, good or bad, without a named, dated, public event.

A card is a vendor graded in this chapter's market. A Play marks a vendor that appears here but is graded in another chapter, where its main product lives. A cross-market ledger tracks every vendor across chapters so no one is counted twice.

My read: marks the author's judgment — opinion, not asserted fact.

The whole report tests one bet through three proxies, set out in full in "The bet, in brief" at the front:

  • P1, one engine: the WFO stack consolidates onto a single AI brain. Strongly evidenced at the product/SKU surface, not as an internal-architecture claim.
  • P2, seat to outcome: pricing moves from per-seat to per-resolution. Observable fact today, on published price lists.
  • P3, the seat absorbs: autonomous agents take the volume a human seat used to handle. A contested forecast.

Read every claim against that split. Price migration is fact; seat elimination is a bet. When this report states a price, it quotes a dated public page. When it discusses displacement, it is weighing a bet.

1.1 What This Market Is

What this market is. Quality & Interaction Analytics is the software that scores and analyzes the content of agent–customer interactions — what was said, by whom, how well, and what it tells the business. It bundles three historically-distinct capabilities now converging on one transcript pipeline. Quality management (QM / automated QA) evaluates an agent's handling of an interaction against a scorecard. Speech & text / interaction analytics transcribes and mines interactions for topics, sentiment, compliance, and root cause. Conversational intelligence is the GenAI-era reframing of both. The category's defining promise in 2026 is the shift from human QA teams sampling ~1–3% of interactions to automated scoring of up to 100% of them.

Where the boundaries fall. The same interaction transcript feeds three markets, so the boundaries are drawn by when and to what end the content is used:

  • vs Recording & Compliance: Recording captures and retains the interaction; this market scores and analyses it. Capture is the input, not the product here.
  • vs Agent-Assist: Agent-Assist acts on the interaction in the live moment (real-time guidance); this market works retrospectively on the completed interaction. When a vendor does both, we classify it by its separately-positioned product line.
  • vs Performance & Engagement: this market produces the score; Performance & Engagement owns what you do with the agent next (coaching, calibration, gamification). The QA→coaching loop is where the two overlap, dissected in Convergence.

What it IS NOT. Not call capture/recording (the Recording & Compliance front). Not forecasting/scheduling (Workforce Management). Not real-time assist (Agent-Assist). Not Voice-of-Customer / CSAT survey platforms (Qualtrics, Medallia, deferred to v1.1; that's the measurement layer above WEM). Not sales/revenue-intelligence (Gong and the like), a different buyer (the revenue org), excluded from this market's scope and sizing.

Three common buyer misconceptions.

  1. "Auto-QA replaces QA analysts." In practice it reallocates them — from sampling-and-scoring to calibration, dispute handling, and coaching design. The headcount story is a shift, not a delete (and where it does delete, we require a named, public event before we say so).
  2. "100% scoring means 100% accuracy." Scoring all interactions is a coverage claim, not an accuracy claim; GenAI auto-scores carry their own error modes and need human calibration — a live buyer-evaluation criterion.
  3. "Speech analytics and quality management are the same product." Related, distinct buying motions: analytics is discovery (what's happening across interactions), QM is evaluation (how well an agent handled one). Many vendors sell both; the buyer often funds them on different lines.

Where the market sits: its own sub-capabilities on two axes, the job done on the transcript (Discovery to Evaluation) against coverage posture (human sampling ~1–3% to automated ~100%), with the adjacent markets drawn as out-of-category boundaries.

1.2 Market Size & Trends

1.2 Market Size & Trends

Category-scoping note. Sized here under the analyst "speech / interaction / conversation analytics" segment definition, the standalone software for capturing, transcribing, and analytically scoring agent–customer interactions, inclusive of the automated-QA layer built on it (the definition used by Grand View, MarketsandMarkets, Mordor, and DMG Consulting). Excluded per the WEM↔Contact Center as a Service (CCaaS) boundary: the recording/capture layer (the Recording & Compliance front), real-time agent-assist (Agent-Assist), revenue-intelligence tools (Gong, different buyer), and the much larger general voice/speech-recognition market.

The spread is the point. Base years span 2023–2026. The 2023 low ($2.82B, Grand View)1 against Mordor's 2026 figure (~$4.0B)3 is a ~42% gap, partly calendar, partly category-boundary. The CAGR band, by contrast, is tight (~15.3–17.5%), and forecasts cluster near $7.3–8.2B in 2029–2031. Longer-horizon outliers (Polaris ~$11.5B/20324; Research&Markets ~$12.0B/20325) roughly double that off a higher base and a longer window; SkyQuest is a low outlier (8.2% CAGR)6. Read it as: analysts broadly agree on a high-teens-growth category landing near $8B by ~2030, but disagree on where the line sits between "interaction analytics" and the broader "conversation-intelligence / Contact Center as a Service AI" blend. That is the WEM↔Contact Center as a Service boundary tension the Convergence chapter exists to dissect. The dispersion is a boundary-definition artifact, not measurement error. (DMG Consulting's proprietary figure sits behind a paywall and is therefore cited for vendor-roster and qualitative framing only, not as a quantitative anchor, disclosed, not estimated.)

Trends that move the buyer. (1) 100%-interaction scoring is now table stakes7, not a differentiator. The value has moved up-stack from "we transcribe your calls" to "we score, coach, and flag every one." (2) GenAI scoring is displacing keyword-spotting (Calabrio markets Auto QM "beyond simple keyword spotting"8; DMG calls the category "a strategic and essential enterprise AI component"9). (3) The QA→coaching loop is the real job-to-be-done. It closes scored-interaction → skill-gap → targeted coaching "without increasing headcount,"10 which pulls the buyer toward the Agent-Assist supervisor. (4) Adoption is broad and AI-led (DMG's Dec-2024 survey: 78% of contact centers already actively engaged with AI)11.

Compliance is decisive here. PCI DSS data-level redaction became mandatory 31 Mar 2025 (disk encryption no longer sufficient)12, pushing redaction into the analytics layer. Routing conversations through third-party LLM APIs creates HIPAA/PCI/GLBA/GDPR/CCPA exposure and a live "where does my interaction data go" objection. And EU AI Act transparency obligations reach full application in August 202612, with AI scoring of agent performance squarely in scope. (Sources: Grand View, MarketsandMarkets, Mordor, DMG, Deepgram, dated 2026-06-16.)

1.4 What the Vendors Are Doing

1.4 What the Vendors Are Doing

Play 1: The single-engine repositioning

Suite vendors recast QM/analytics as outputs of one cross-market AI engine rather than standalone SKUs (NICE Enlighten "at the core of everything"13; Verint open platform15; Genesys Cloud AI Studio17). Best-of-breed is pushed to defend on depth + independence + compliance. Feeds Convergence P1.

Play 2: The race to ~100% automated evaluation

Every tier publicly claims near-total coverage (Verint Quality Bot "up to 96%,"16 Observe.AI/Cresta/Genesys/NICE "100%"). Likely outcome: coverage commoditizes by end-2026; differentiation migrates to calibration trust, bias defensibility, and coaching action.

Play 3: Two consolidation engines pull in opposite directions

Thoma Bravo's PE roll-up (Verint+Calabrio)14 vs NICE's organic/acqui-suite build vs Genesys' Contact Center as a Service bundle. Verint+Calabrio product-line de-duplication is the watch-item through H1 2027.

Play 4: The acquisition sweep thins the independent bench

Echo AI → Calabrio (Dec 2024), Loris → Contentsquare (Jul 2025)24, VOCALLS → CallMiner (Jun 2025). Genuine independent Wildcards are now scarce — the thin Wildcard tier is itself a signal.

Play 5: Adjacent-suite reach-in

Conversational-AI/VoC players extend evaluation into this market — Uniphore (agentic overlay)25, Sprinklr ("Autonomous Evaluation" of AI-agent behavior, Spring '26)26, Five9 Agentic QM (controlled availability 1Q 2026)27. As AI agents take tier-1 volume, "quality" expands from evaluating humans to evaluating AI agents, a Quality→Autonomous CX blur and a Convergence input.

1.6 Winning & Losing

1.6 Winning & Losing

The three pattern-claim callouts below are my read of where this market is heading. Each is anchored to public evidence already cited in this chapter, framed explicitly as opinion, and put as a falsifiable prediction with a dated checkpoint an outsider can watch.

Pattern Claim 1 — The Auto-QA Reallocation Thesis

Observation. Every tier now markets automated scoring of ~100% of interactions, all on dated 2026 vendor pages: Verint Quality Bot "up to 96% of calls"16; Observe.AI "100% … objective, consistent, and free of bias"20; Cresta "100% … at lower cost versus manual sampling"21; NICE/Genesys "100% of interactions."1713
My read. Once everyone can score everything, "100% coverage" stops differentiating; the contest moves to calibration trust, bias defensibility, and whether the score actually drives coaching. The human-in-the-loop control becomes the pitch, not the coverage number — and the QA analyst is reallocated to calibration and coaching, not deleted.
Conditional prediction. If by Q4 2026 the leading vendors' QM hero-messaging has shifted its headline away from coverage percentages toward calibration / bias-defensibility / coaching-outcome language, the reallocation thesis holds; if "100%" is still the hero number, it has not.
Sources. Vendor QM/analytics pages, accessed 2026-06-16: verint.com, observe.ai, cresta.com, nice.com, genesys.com.

Claim 1 — The Auto-QA reallocation: once every tier scores ~100%, coverage commoditizes and the contest moves to calibration trust, bias defensibility, and coaching outcome; the analyst is reallocated, not deleted.

Pattern Claim 2 — The Independent-Depth Defense

Observation. Three suites position QM/analytics as outputs of one cross-market AI engine (NICE "purpose-built AI at the core of everything we do"13; Verint "the value has shifted to the intelligence layer"15; Genesys native "100% of conversations"17; vendor pages accessed 2026-06-16). The independents (CallMiner, Observe.AI, Cresta) defend on analytics depth, regulated-industry compliance, and independence from any single Contact Center as a Service stack.
My read. The suites win the "one bill, pre-integrated" buyer; the independents win the depth / regulated / multi-vendor buyer. This is the market-level instance of the report's central question — and the Thoma Bravo Verint+Calabrio combination complicates it by making the largest "independent" itself a PE-consolidated platform.
Conditional prediction. If through H1 2027 independent best-of-breed analytics vendors still win the majority of net-new competitive displacements in regulated-vertical enterprise deals, depth-and-independence remains a durable defense; if suite-bundled QM takes the majority, the single-engine collapse is real at the market level.
Sources. Vendor platform pages, accessed 2026-06-16: nice.com, verint.com, genesys.com, callminer.com, observe.ai, cresta.com.

Claim 2 — The independent-depth defense: suites win the pre-integrated buyer, independents hold the depth / regulated / multi-vendor buyer, and Thoma Bravo CX complicates the line as a PE-consolidated "independent."

Pattern Claim 3 — The Vanishing Wildcard

Observation. The independent QA-AI middle is being absorbed before it reaches scale (Echo AI → Calabrio, Dec 2024; Loris → Contentsquare, Jul 202524; VOCALLS → CallMiner, Jun 2025); a deliberate sweep of the vendor universe surfaced only one genuinely-independent, genuinely-thin Wildcard (OttoQA, seed-stage)23.
My read. The scarcity of the Wildcard tier is itself the signal — acquirers are buying the QA-AI middle before it consolidates into Gravity, thinning the independent bench that the depth-defense (Claim 2) relies on.
Conditional prediction. If by mid-2027 at least two more current Attention-tier independents are acquired into a suite or PE platform, the standalone QA-AI category is consolidating toward a suite-vs-platform structure; if new independently-funded entrants reach Series B in the space, the bench is replenishing.
Sources. The acquisition record set out in Funding & Fault-Lines and What the Vendors Are Doing: Echo AI → Calabrio (Dec 2024), Loris → Contentsquare (Jul 2025), VOCALLS → CallMiner (Jun 2025).

Claim 3 — The vanishing wildcard: the independent QA-AI middle is absorbed before it reaches scale, leaving OttoQA the lone genuinely-thin Wildcard.

The Verdict — Winners, Watch &...

The Verdict — Winners, Watch & Losers

Winners

NICE, Genesys, and Thoma Bravo CX. In my read, the three Gravity suites are pulling ahead on integration convenience — the "one bill, pre-integrated" buyer who wants quality management delivered as an output of a connected platform, not a standalone tool. Their Gravity placement in The Contenders and the single-engine repositioning in What the Vendors Are Doing are the anchors. NICE and Genesys, whose native 100%-interaction scoring is the clearest expression of that pitch, are the ones to beat for that buyer.

CallMiner, Observe.AI, and Cresta. I see the strongest independents holding the depth line — the regulated, best-of-breed, multi-vendor buyer at the center of the independent-depth defense. CallMiner's hybrid multi-technique posture and regulated-industry depth, Observe.AI's auto-QA focus, and Cresta's gen-AI-native positioning (cresta.com, accessed 2026-06-16)21, all Attention-tier cards in The Contenders, are what that buyer is buying.

Watch

OttoQA. I'd watch the self-serve, transparent-pricing entrant opening the un-served small contact-center segment. It is a different game than the enterprise contest, and the one place in this market where pricing is actually public (its Wildcard card and pricing note in The Contenders are the anchors). Its viability stays a strategy read only, not a survival call.

Level AI. I see the most likely next fold-in — a growth-stage Attention-tier independent of exactly the profile the acquisition sweep in What the Vendors Are Doing keeps absorbing. A deal here would confirm the thinning bench flagged in The Road Ahead.

Losers

I am not naming a loser in this market, and I will not manufacture one. Funding & Fault-Lines found no named, dated, public distress event for any contender. What the record shows instead is consolidation, not failure: the independent QA-AI middle being bought before it reaches scale (Echo AI into Calabrio, Dec 2024; Loris into Contentsquare, Jul 202524; VOCALLS into CallMiner, Jun 2025). Those are acquisitions of capable assets, not exits under duress, and the vendors whose fate is genuinely contested sit under Watch, not here.
