How to read this report

The report grades five markets. Each chapter grades up to eight vendors (the cap), grouped in three tiers by market weight:

  • Gravity: the largest vendors. Their installed base pulls the market.
  • Attention: the credible mid-sized contenders worth tracking.
  • Wildcard: the newest or least-documented entrant graded. The tier measures the depth of the public record, not the company's health; the report asserts no vendor's viability, good or bad, without a named, dated, public event.

A card is a vendor graded in this chapter's market. A Play marks a vendor that appears here but is graded in another chapter, where its main product lives. A cross-market ledger tracks every vendor across chapters so no one is counted twice.

My read: marks the author's judgment — opinion, not asserted fact.

The whole report tests one bet through three proxies, set out in full in "The bet, in brief" at the front:

  • P1, one engine: the WFO stack consolidates onto a single AI brain. Strongly evidenced at the product/SKU surface, not as an internal-architecture claim.
  • P2, seat to outcome: pricing moves from per-seat to per-resolution. Observable fact today, on published price lists.
  • P3, the seat absorbs: autonomous agents take the volume a human seat used to handle. A contested forecast.

Read every claim against that split. Price migration is fact; seat elimination is a bet. When this report states a price, it quotes a dated public page. When it discusses displacement, it is weighing a bet.

4.1 What This Market Is

What this market is. Agent-Assist & Real-Time Guidance is the software that changes the outcome of a conversation while it is happening. It covers real-time guidance and nudges, in-the-moment knowledge surfacing, next-best-action, live compliance prompts, and the after-call-work (auto-summary, disposition) that closes the interaction. It is the live moment of the WEM stack: where Quality & Interaction Analytics scores the interaction after and Performance & Engagement coaches the agent later, this market acts during. It also absorbs the buyer-visible half of knowledge management (knowledge-in-the-flow), folded in here.

The buyer is the live moment: the supervisor/CX-ops leader who wants the agent to say the right thing, find the right answer, and finish the after-call work without a hold, on this call.

Where the boundaries fall — and this is the report's sharpest one.

  • vs Quality & Analytics: Quality & Interaction Analytics is retrospective (score the completed interaction); this market is real-time (act during it). Same transcript, different time.
  • vs Performance & Engagement: Performance & Engagement develops the agent between calls (coaching, gamification); this market guides them on the call.
  • vs Autonomous CX, the control-surface boundary: This is the line the whole report turns on. Assist returns a suggestion to a person who acts; autonomous is the resolution itself, the AI takes the action, the human is out of the loop. The discriminator is who performs the resolving action, not how capable the AI is, and a human-approval gate is what keeps a system in Agent-Assist. Vendors draw this line themselves, with two separately-named SKUs on one engine (NICE Copilot vs Autopilot; Cresta Agent Assist vs AI Agent; Genesys Copilot vs autonomous agents; Five9 assist vs agents that "Reason, Decide, and Take Action"). That is the single-engine thesis made physical: one engine, two SKUs, gated by whether a human is in the loop (see the Winning & Losing section below and the Convergence chapter).

What it IS NOT. Not post-interaction QM (Quality & Interaction Analytics). Not between-call coaching/gamification (Performance & Engagement). Not autonomous deflection / virtual agents that resolve without a human (Autonomous CX — the other side of the control-surface line). Not standalone enterprise knowledge management (only the in-the-flow, buyer-visible half lives here).

Three common buyer misconceptions.

  1. "Agent assist is live transcription." Transcription is table-stakes; the product is the guidance on top — what the agent should say or do next, grounded in something (top-performer behaviors, your own data).
  2. "A copilot and an autonomous agent are different products." Increasingly they are the same engine exposed as two SKUs separated only by a human-approval gate — which is why the assist vendor and the autonomous vendor are usually the same vendor.
  3. "More AI guidance is always better." The mature pitch is calibrated guidance (Five9 explicitly suppresses prompts for seasoned agents "so as not to annoy them") and trusted guidance (explainability/evaluation is the 2026 enterprise gate).

4.2 Market Size & Trends

4.2 Market Size & Trends

Category-scoping note. Sized as real-time assist to a human agent (live guidance, knowledge-in-the-flow, next-best-action, auto-summary/ACW). In scope: the copilot/guidance layer a human agent uses live. Out of scope: autonomous deflection / virtual agents that resolve without a human (Autonomous CX — the control-surface line), and post-interaction QM (Quality & Interaction Analytics). This is the newest and least cleanly-sized WEM-core market. Agent-assist is still being separated from the broader Contact Center as a Service (CCaaS) AI blend it was born inside.

Market size: blended, single-source-thin, flagged not laundered. Exactly one widely-circulated source names "real-time agent assist" as its own category (market.us: ~$4.4B/2024 → $124.6B/2034 at ~40% CAGR)1 — reported but flagged hard for single-source + improbably-hot growth, not dressed as settled. The directional anchor is the better-corroborated "call center AI" blend (Grand View, Fortune, + labeled outliers)23, with every figure marked blended, since agent-assist is one line item inside it. There is no clean standalone dollar TAM here, and saying so is more credible than a fabricated point.

Trends that move the buyer. (1) Auto-summary / after-call-work automation is the measurable killer app — vendor-claimed Five9 ~40% of agent time4, Verint ~30 sec/call5 — the wedge that proves ROI before autonomous. (2) Real-time RAG knowledge-surfacing (the buyer-visible half of KM) becomes a default assist capability, thinning standalone in-agent KM.6 (3) Next-best-action whisper guidance grounded in top-performer behavior.7 (4) The assist→autonomous gradient is the structural story: Gartner's "80% autonomous by 2029"8 pull vs the counter-signal that a large share of agentic projects get canceled9 — both feeding the Convergence falsification condition.

Compliance. Real-time processing puts customer data in the live loop, so the HIPAA/PCI/GDPR exposure from Quality & Interaction Analytics applies in real time. On top of that, the EU AI Act's prohibition on workplace emotion/sentiment recognition (Art. 5(1)(f)) bites real-time agent-sentiment scoring specifically (the live version of the Agent-Assist emotion-AI wall — binding the EU deploying employer's use, narrow medical/safety carve-outs aside), with full application Aug 2026.10

Play 4: Real-time assist becomes the on-ramp to autonomous CX

Cresta Agent Assist beside AI Agent; Balto assist beside Togo; Five9 assist inside the Genius agentic motion. The "guide the human" engine and the "be the agent" engine converge — pulling Agent-Assist into Autonomous CX, where the seat question begins.

Play 5: "Trust the copilot" becomes the enterprise gate, via evaluation + explainability

Sprinklr "Autonomous Evaluation… explainable logs"20; Cresta "Responsible AI." The same evaluation lens that validates a copilot validates an autonomous agent.

4.6 Winning & Losing

4.6 Winning & Losing

These are my read of where this market is heading — each anchored to public evidence already cited above, framed as opinion, and stated as a falsifiable prediction with a dated checkpoint.

Pattern Claim 1 — The Control-Surface, Not the Engine

Observation. The same vendors sell real-time assist and autonomous CX as two separately-named SKUs on one platform, gated by whether a human acts. The pairs are explicit: NICE Copilot vs Autopilot11; Cresta Agent Assist ("guides… without interacting with the customer directly") vs AI Agent13; Genesys Copilot vs autonomous agents12; Five9 Agent Assist vs agents that "Reason, Decide, and Take Action"19 (dated vendor pages, accessed 2026-06-16).
My read. The Agent-Assist / Autonomous CX boundary is a control-surface boundary (who performs the resolving action), not an engine boundary — vendors run one engine and expose it as two products separated by a human-approval gate. This is the single-engine thesis made physical, and it is also the pricing hinge: the assist SKU keeps a human seat; the autonomous SKU removes it.
Conditional prediction. If by Q4 2026 at least 3 Gravity-tier vendors publicly position assist and autonomous as one platform with a human-in-the-loop gate (rather than two separate products), the control-surface thesis is confirmed. If instead they keep them architecturally separate, the single-engine collapse is weaker than the marketing implies.
Sources. The dated vendor SKU pages quoted in the Contenders section (NICE Copilot/Autopilot, Cresta Agent Assist/AI Agent, Genesys, Five9; accessed 2026-06-16) and the control-surface boundary set out in the What This Market Is and What the Vendors Are Doing sections above.

Figure 4.6a — One engine exposed as an assist SKU (Copilot) or an autonomous SKU (Autopilot), gated by whether a human is in the loop. The falsifiable test is boxed at the foot of the figure.

Pattern Claim 2 — Auto-Summary Is the Beachhead

Observation. After-call-work automation (auto-summary, disposition) is the market's measurable ROI wedge, vendor-claimed Five9 ~40% of agent time4 and Verint ~30 sec/call5, and it ships as a standard assist capability across NICE, Genesys, Five9, Uniphore.
My read. ACW automation is the beachhead that proves assist ROI before the buyer trusts autonomous resolution — it is low-risk (post-call, human-reviewed), quantifiable, and universal. The vendor that wins ACW credibility earns the right to sell the rest of the copilot, then the autonomous SKU.
Conditional prediction. If by H1 2027 auto-summary/ACW automation is a standard, default-on capability across all Gravity assist products (not an add-on), it has commoditized as the table-stakes beachhead and differentiation has moved up to live guidance + autonomy. If it is still sold as a premium add-on, the ROI case is not yet settled.
Sources. Market Size & Trends section's after-call-work figures (vendor-claimed Five9 ~40% of agent time, Verint ~30 sec/call) and Plays 1 and 3 above.

Figure 4.6b — After-call-work automation is the low-risk, measurable ROI wedge the buyer trusts before live guidance and, last, autonomous resolution. The vendor-claimed proof points and the falsifiable test are carried in the figure.

Pattern Claim 3 — The Seat Question Starts at This Boundary

Observation. Assist keeps the human seat, because it guides a person; the same vendors' autonomous SKUs remove it (Cresta AI Agent, Balto Togo14, NICE Autopilot, Five9 agentic CX). Pricing is starting to follow: autonomous-CX vendors publicly list per-resolution/outcome pricing while assist stays per-seat.
My read. The per-seat → per-outcome pricing migration the Convergence chapter tracks physically begins at the Agent-Assist / Autonomous-CX control-surface line — the moment the engine that guided the human starts replacing the human, the unit being priced stops being a seat. Agent-Assist is therefore the earliest observable site where the human seat count starts to fall.
Conditional prediction. If by mid-2027 at least 2 Gravity-tier vendors price their assist SKU per-seat but their autonomous SKU per-resolution/outcome on the same platform, the pricing hinge is confirmed at this boundary. If autonomous stays per-seat-priced, the seat-reset thesis is delayed.
Sources. Play 4 above and the control-surface boundary in the What This Market Is section; the per-resolution pricing evidence carried in the Autonomous CX chapter.

The Verdict — Winners, Watch &...

The Verdict — Winners, Watch & Losers

Winners Translating the tier placements and plays above into a verdict, my opinion, not a scoreboard. NICE, Genesys, Five9. They own the live moment as one surface of a single-engine platform (their Gravity-tier cards above); assist is a module of the AI platform the buyer already runs, not a separate purchase. Cresta. The independent that earns a second card, on generative-AI depth and named-outlet "under 200 ms" latency, the clearest depth-and-latency answer to the suites. Balto. The assist-primary independent, built assist-first rather than added onto a QM / Contact Center as a Service core.

Watch The control-surface line itself: whether assist and autonomous stay one human-gated platform or fork into separate products (Pattern Claim 1's Q4 2026 checkpoint). This single question decides the report's single-engine thesis, and Agent-Assist is the earliest place it is observable. Uniphore. The heavily-funded scale outlier (~$2.5B — set at its Feb 2022 Series E, held flat at its Oct 2025 $260M Series F) whose assist is one module of a broad suite; worth watching for whether that capital converts into live-moment leadership.

Losers No graded contender here carries a public distress or failure signal — the Funding & Fault-Lines section found no distress event for any of the eight, so I name none rather than manufacture one. The clearest restructuring evidence in this market is absorption, not failure: the standalone assist/emotion-AI capability folding into a suite (Cogito absorbed into Verint, now Thoma Bravo CX, for $38.2M)22, and, per three of the plays above, knowledge-in-the-flow collapsing the standalone in-agent knowledge-management surface. I read that as consolidation of the category, not the death of a vendor.
