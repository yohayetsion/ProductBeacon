Open-Source Intelligence (OSINT / WEBINT)

5.1 The Playing Ground

Open-source intelligence is the discipline of exploiting information that is already in the open — and the defining line is that the access is unprivileged.

  • What it IS. Collection and exploitation of openly-available, web-resident, social, and dark-web data — scrapers, dark-web crawlers, social harvesters, multilingual entity extraction over web text. The collector is what gets graded here.
  • The defining property. A vendor or its customer reaches the source without a court order, a carrier tap, or a deployed device. That single property separates OSINT from every intercept front — and it is why "open" is the word most prone to category sprawl.
  • What it is NOT. Not intercepted content (the privileged-access fronts), not the fusion layer that ingests OSINT, not proprietary cyber-threat feeds. The grading test: is the buyer paying for the collection engine, or for analytics over data the customer supplies?
  • The buyer. National-intelligence and national-security agencies, law-enforcement investigations units, defense and military-intelligence cells, corporate-security and threat-intelligence functions, and financial-crime and due-diligence teams.
  • Three buyer misconceptions. "OSINT is free — it's all on Google" (the raw material is free; scale, dark-web access, 200+ languages, entity-resolution, and provenance are the product). "OSINT and SOCMINT are the same" (SOCMINT is a subset of the whole open web). "If it's public, collecting it is legal" (terms-of-service and data-protection law constrain how public data is gathered — and the constraint has teeth).

How to read this market

Each chapter grades up to eight vendors (the cap of eight), grouped in three tiers:

  • Gravity: the structural anchors a buyer cannot map the front without naming.
  • Attention: the specialists shaping the front's 2026 demand.
  • Wildcard: graded at the edge of the rubric and held there with explicit caution; the read stays descriptive and hedged.

My read. marks the author's judgment. Each pattern claim opens with a dated public observation, separates that observation from my read of it, and ends with a conditional a publicly observable proxy can confirm or refute before the next refresh.

Every vendor is graded on a dated public pillar; capability is reported as public positioning, never as verified architecture.

5.2 The Terrain

There is no single number for the OSINT market. The honest presentation sets the public estimates side by side and lets the spread speak — the disagreement is the finding.

  • The three bracket 2025 at roughly USD 12.7B–18.2B; the growth rates split — Mordor's mid-teens (it excludes threat-hunting and hardware sensors) against the two high-twenties figures (which fold dark-web analysis in). An unsettled category boundary, not a counting error.
  • The government slice is a labeled proxy. Government intelligence agencies are 38.40% of 2025 OSINT revenue (Mordor)1; national security is 32% (GMI)3. Bracketing arithmetic implies roughly USD 4–7B for 2025 — a share-of-market, not a disclosed segment size.
  • Named procurement is the harder currency. The FBI bought up to USD 27M of Babel Street licenses (2022)4; the DoD PADELS award to Dataminr ran USD 24.9M, which Babel Street and Dataminr litigated at the GAO5 — a recompeted, contestable lane.
  • Demand drivers. The open-data exhaust has outrun manual analysis; government buyers are institutionalizing OSINT as a named discipline (the CIA's public OSINT strategy enlists large language models)6; and a commercial base below the government buyers funds the push into government tenders.

Gravity tier — the structural anchors

The three names a buyer cannot map the front without.

  • Recorded Future — the category's structural anchor, publicly described as "the world's largest threat intelligence company," with 1,900+ clients across 75 countries including 45 governments.7 Its USP is breadth-of-collection into a single Intelligence Graph. The front's most consequential fact sits here: Mastercard acquired it for USD 2.65B (announced September 2024, closed December 2024).8 The anchor is no longer independent.
  • Dataminr — the speed archetype: earliest signal across "1M public data sources,"9 marketing among the most AI-native stacks in the front. In March 2026 it launched "Dataminr for Cyber Defense," a suite "built with agentic and predictive AI at its core"10 — extending up the stack toward the fusion/analytics layer.
  • Babel Street — the multilingual-and-licensed-data anchor, leading its 2026 framing with "Agentic Risk Intelligence" and "governed AI agents on rights-cleared, mission-grade data."11 An aggressive federal-and-AI hire cycle (John Larson, ex-Booz Allen, President and Chief AI Officer, December 2025)12. Ownership is private and undisclosed.

Attention tier — demand, M&A, and the AI-native narrative

The four shaping 2026 demand and the architecture-disruption story.

  • Flashpoint — the primary-source-depth contender: direct access to closed adversary spaces "that others can't reach,"13 with human-analyst enrichment as an explicit pillar. AI added as summarization over a primary-source data lake. Its wedge is depth-over-breadth.
  • Cognyte (LUMINAR) — graded strictly on its publicly-marketed external/web-intelligence product, LUMINAR ("AI-powered external threat intelligence," deep/dark-web monitoring), plus the NEXYTE decision-intelligence platform. Sourced entirely to dated public pages, with no internal knowledge; it sits squarely on the collector-or-fusion boundary this front turns on.
  • Maltego — the analyst-workbench archetype: the graph-first link-analysis surface other data providers plug into, "the most widely used graph analysis platform for OSINT in the world."14 Graded on architectural distinctness, not as a data feed.
  • Fivecast — the clearest mission-built-for-government collector, "Government agencies (40+)," pursuing a partnership stack (Tranquility AI, March 2026)15 rather than M&A to reach a full collect-to-evidence workflow.

Wildcard tier — Strider, and the cut line

Strider reframes what OSINT is for.

  • Strider Technologies — markets nation-state and economic-espionage risk derived from public data: "unparalleled insights into state-sponsored threats," a "zero-touch architecture requiring no internal client data" 16 across "90K+ hard-to-access sources."17 A USD 55M Series C led by Pelion (September 2024), roughly USD 117M total.18 The bet: does "strategic intelligence" harden into a category that pulls net-new budget, or stay a well-funded vertical?
  • The cut line. Eight more public candidates are placed, not graded: Cybersixgill (folds into Flashpoint's depth pillar), ZeroFox (an ownership event — taken private by Haveli for ~USD 350M)19, PenLink / Cobwebs (LEA tooling, litigation-sensitive on location data), Primer, Social Links (largely a data provider inside Maltego), ShadowDragon, and Skopenow.
  • Voyager Labs sits in the never-graded surveillance set — descriptive market-force commentary only, with no grade, no pattern claim, and no distress label.

5.4 Their Plays

Five dated moves: two consolidation plays, one positioning convergence, two growth bets.

  • Absorbed into a payments network. Mastercard bought Recorded Future for USD 2.65B from Insight Partners20 — folded toward fraud, identity, and real-time decisioning.
  • PE rolls up the LEA suite. Cobwebs joined PenLink (Estimate: ~USD 200M, CTech; terms undisclosed, both under Spire Capital)2122; ZeroFox went private with Haveli (~USD 350M).23 The Texas DPS Tangles contract (June 2025) proves the demand curve is still live.24
  • Partnership stacks over M&A. Fivecast and Tranquility AI partnered (March 2026) to build the collect-to-evidence workflow by alliance, not balance sheet.25
  • A nation-state wedge draws net-new budget. Strider's Series C funds a vertical the generalists don't own26; Babel Street is building toward the same wedge.27

The fifth move — the front-wide "agentic AI" repositioning — is treated as its own pattern claim below.

Pattern Claim 1 — The Strategic-Acquirer Absorption Thesis

  • Observation. In eighteen months the front lost three independents to acquirers and a fourth agreed to roll up a fifth: Mastercard–Recorded Future (USD 2.65B), Bitsight–Cybersixgill (~USD 115M), Haveli–ZeroFox (~USD 350M), PenLink–Cobwebs (Estimate: ~USD 200M, CTech; terms undisclosed), and Dataminr's USD 290M ThreatConnect deal.3637
  • My read. The independent-brand path is closing. The buyers are no longer intelligence specialists — they are payments networks, security-ratings platforms, and PE control vehicles. Survivors are either acquisition candidates or specialists too narrow to interest a generalist acquirer.
  • Conditional. Hardens on a second non-defense-platform acquisition of a pure-play, or a further roll-up. Weakens if a pure-play raises a fresh named-lead mega-round at an up valuation, or Recorded Future's government roster contracts post-close.

Pattern Claim 2 — The Agentic Repositioning

  • Observation. In 2025–2026 the contenders converged on near-identical "agentic AI" language: Dataminr ("agentic and predictive AI at its core"), Babel Street ("Agentic Risk Intelligence"), Strider ("Powered by Agentic Intelligence"38), and Cognyte's NEXYTE ("agentic decision flows"). Maltego and Flashpoint add AI as bolt-on summarization.
  • My read. A positioning convergence, not a verified capability gap — true architecture cannot be verified from outside. What is real is the funding earmarked for it (Dataminr's April-2025 round states the purpose verbatim39) and the simultaneity. When every hero page carries "agentic" in the same quarter, the word signals table-stakes, not a moat.
  • Conditional. Separates into a real differentiator if one vendor publishes a named-reference customer attributing an outcome to autonomous agentic action and the others cannot. If it stays uniform marketing with no named-outcome win through 2026, the pillar demotes to a watchlist item.

Pattern Claim 3 — The Contestable-Lane-and-Free-Floor Squeeze

  • Observation. U.S. government OSINT is bought through commercial-off-the-shelf subscriptions on federal vehicles (Babel Street on Carahsoft's GSA Schedule)40, openly recompeted and litigated (the Babel Street–Dataminr GAO protest41; the FBI's 5,000-license buy)42. Underneath sits a compressing free floor — Maltego community editions, open-source toolkits, general-purpose LLMs — with government agencies still the largest end-user segment at 38.40% of 2025 revenue.
  • My read. A vise on the middle of the market. Accessible procurement is why the front overflows and consolidates so fast; the same accessibility means the low end is eaten from below. Pricing power stays with vendors who climb up-stack — investigative-grade multilingual exploitation, maintained dark/deep-web depth, defensible provenance, accredited deployment.
  • Conditional. Holds if H2 2026 shows continued recompeted, contested awards on GSA/Carahsoft-class vehicles. The "government edge is carved out" read weakens if a major vendor loses a government contract or draws a regulator order tied to public-data scraping in 2026.

5.6 Winning & Losing

The verdict rests on disclosed public events, framed as opinion. The throughline: the independent brand is being absorbed, every survivor is making the agentic pivot, and a contestable lane with a free floor below decides who keeps pricing power.

  • Winners — Recorded Future, Dataminr, Strider. Recorded Future sold at the top of the cycle (USD 2.65B to Mastercard, the largest disclosed transaction here — scoped to the deal, not post-close execution). Dataminr is the lone independent acting as consolidator rather than target (USD 100M Fortress financing, the USD 290M ThreatConnect deal — caveat: last priced at USD 4.1B in 2021). Strider is winning a net-new vertical, not the incumbent fight (USD 55M Series C funding the nation-state wedge).
  • Watch — Babel Street, Fivecast. Babel Street is an active build-and-hire platform with documented Veritas backing at the time of the January 2024 Vertical Knowledge acquisition, while current ownership remains unresolved — watch for a current-owner disclosure or fresh named-lead primary round. Fivecast is the clearest government-mission pure-play, betting on a partnership stack — watch for a named government reference win, or the stack getting acquired.
  • Losers — none graded. The public events on this front are consolidation and strategic exit, not failure. ZeroFox's take-private sits below SPAC-era levels but is a PE de-listing, not a collapse; Voyager Labs and the NSO/Candiru/Intellexa cohort are litigation-sensitive and never graded. Quarterly refreshes will populate this line if an OSINT-specific distress signal emerges.

5.7 The Campaign Ahead

Five watchlist items for H2 2026 — each naming a public, observable threshold that would trigger re-assessment.

  • A second non-defense-platform acquisition of an OSINT pure-play — or a further roll-up after the closed Dataminr/ThreatConnect deal — hardens the absorption thesis into the front's defining structure.
  • A named-outcome agentic reference win from Dataminr, Babel Street, Strider, or Cognyte — attributing a decision to autonomous agentic action, separating the agentic claims from category-wide marketing.
  • A scraping-law action against a government-facing OSINT vendor — a regulator order or lost contract tied to public-data scraping — weakening the "government edge is carved out" read.
  • Recorded Future's government franchise post-close — retained named references confirm the acquirer preserved it; visible contraction signals integration strain.
  • A generalist answering the nation-state / supply-chain wedge — validating Strider's category, or Strider's next round stalling and the wedge proving narrower than the raise implied.
