1.1 The Playing Ground

1.1 The Playing Ground

National-agency SIGINT is the top of the collection stack: an agency collecting and analysing communications at the scale of a country's network backbone, standing, continuously, and remotely. The sensor is wired into the infrastructure itself. The buyer is a national-security, national-intelligence, or military-intelligence agency that needs to see, profile, and relation-map a population of communications as infrastructure. The boundary has to be drawn with precision, because the technology rhymes across fronts even as the legal posture and scale do not: this front is strategic, standing, mass-scale passive collection and analytics at national-backbone scale — defined by volume plus standing infrastructure, not by any single technology it touches.

  • The defining property is volume plus standing infrastructure — a passive probe at a carrier core, an international gateway, or an IXP, sized in terabits per second and petabytes of retention. The vendors name it themselves ("terabit-speed," "petabyte-scale," "national networks")1; the agency-build record names the same architecture — NSA "upstream collection" feeding XKEYSCORE.23
  • Versus the Law Enforcement Monitoring Centers: the bright line is the legal trigger plus the scale, not the technology. A monitoring centre receives intercept through the carrier, under a warrant, for a named target. This front collects without a per-target warrant, at population scale, standing. DPI is a technology, not a front — it lives in both.
  • Versus Tactical Intelligence: that front is field, proximity, portable collection against a present target. This front collects remotely, at the backbone — strategic collection is fixed and continuous where tactical is portable and present-tense.
  • Versus Fusion / Decision Intelligence: a mass-collection platform with a built-in dashboard is graded here, because its analytics are inseparable from its own collection. A source-agnostic fusion platform sold standalone is graded there.
  • Above every vendor sits the in-house build — the NSA-class sovereign program — carried as the front's non-vendor Tier-0 force.

How to read this market

Each chapter grades up to eight vendors (the cap of eight), grouped in three tiers:

  • Gravity: the structural anchors a buyer cannot map the front without naming.
  • Attention: the specialists shaping the front's 2026 demand.
  • Wildcard: graded at the edge of the rubric and held there with explicit caution; the read stays descriptive and hedged.

My read. marks the author's judgment. Each pattern claim opens with a dated public observation, separates that observation from my read of it, and ends with a conditional a publicly observable proxy can confirm or refute before the next refresh.

Every vendor is graded on a dated public pillar; capability is reported as public positioning, never as verified architecture.

The Encryption Story: From Ins...

The Encryption Story: From Inspection to Inference

The buyer who thinks a backbone tap is a national wiretap is buying yesterday's network. The single most important technical fact in this front is that the reliable, durable yield of backbone collection is migrating from content toward metadata, classification, and behavioural inference — because transport encryption now defends most content end to end.

  • TLS 1.3 mandates Perfect Forward Secrecy, so passive out-of-band decryption is no longer possible.4 QUIC moves the handshake into encrypted UDP. Encrypted Client Hello hides the last cleartext label, the Server Name Indication.5
  • Every serious DPI vendor has repositioned around this — from "inspect content" to ML-based encrypted-traffic intelligence that fingerprints flows by behaviour.
  • Crypto de-anonymization at national scale is treated strictly as vendor positioning where it appears, never asserted — it carries the widest gap between marketing and outside-verifiability, and the encryption headwind cuts directly against it.

The Stack: Four Layers on the ...

The Stack: Four Layers on the Backbone

The national buyer assembles four layers, unified by the fact that the sensor is passive, wired into the backbone, and sized for volume. Each maps to a publicly observable proxy rather than an unverifiable capability claim.

  • Bulk access and capture at the carrier core, gateway, or IXP — terabit-speed, petabyte-scale. The passive probe is the architectural signature.
  • DPI and metadata extraction — classify the application, then extract. Sustaining "every packet" at carrier line rate is the genuinely hard, capital-intensive table-stakes.
  • Encrypted-traffic intelligence — the ML pivot as cleartext disappears. This is the defining technical contest of 2026: who holds classification accuracy as the labels vanish.
  • Mass profiling and relation analytics over the collected population — the point where the front most blurs into Fusion, resolved by the inseparable-from-own-collection test.

The Barriers: Export Control I...

The Barriers: Export Control Is the Gate

This front's defining barrier is not technology — it is export control, which doubles as the legitimacy frame for the whole national-surveillance discussion. Any control-classification claim about a named vendor cites the regime text or the vendor's own words, never an analyst assertion.

  • Wassenaar controls IP-network surveillance under Category 5 Part 1 (Telecommunications) — distinct from the intrusion-software line under Category 4 that governs the mobile-implant class carved out elsewhere.6 The cross-front boundary is encoded in the control taxonomy.
  • US EAR ECCN 5A001.j is, in effect, a regulator's specification of a National-Agencies system: "carrier class… national grade IP backbone," application-layer analysis, hard-selector search, relational-network mapping.78
  • EU Dual-Use Regulation 2021/821, Article 5 adds a human-rights end-use catch-all reaching non-listed cyber-surveillance items.
  • The enforcement edge is real and named — the Sandvine case: Entity-List addition (Feb 2024) over documented mass-web-monitoring, removal (Oct 2024) after reforms, rebrand to AppLogic Networks (Mar 2025).910 Every negative is attributed to an official record or named outlet.

The three regimes — Wassenaar Category 5 Part 1, US EAR ECCN 5A001.j, and EU Dual-Use Article 5 — that gate who may sell mass-collection systems and to whom. Conceptual, not data-derived. Not investment advice.

1.2 The Terrain

1.2 The Terrain

There is no clean public dollar figure for this front's mass-collection slice, and the honest way to size it is to set the adjacent markets side by side and let the spread between definitions speak. National mass-collection budgets are opaque or classified; the specialists are mostly private. The thin-material result is expected and honest, not a gap to fill by invention.

  • Four walled markets, never summed (summing multiply-counts the same vendors): lawful interception (~$5.5B–8.2B, blended with the Monitoring Centers), deep-packet inspection (~$5.7B–38B, a six-to-seven-fold publisher spread, overwhelmingly commercial), SIGINT (~$17.7B–18.5B, prime-and-platform-dominated), telecom/network analytics (~$4.1B–9.3B, commercial).
  • The two hardest public anchors are the issuers who disclose: Allot (NASDAQ/TASE: ALLT) at ~$102M FY2025 revenue, first GAAP profit in over a decade11 — evidence the technology base is healthy, not a government-collection size; BAE Systems (LSE: BA.L) Cyber & Intelligence segment at £2.4B12, the SIGINT sub-line not broken out.
  • Demand drivers: the encryption arms race compressing passive inspection; cyber-sovereignty and data-localization mandates forcing in-country infrastructure; the 5G-standalone core refresh; and the build-versus-buy fault line capping commercial reach at the top tier.

1.3 The Contenders

This is the deepest collection front on the demand side and one of the most opaque on the supply side. Grading turns on a single boundary test applied without exception: a vendor grades here only if its own dated public pages market mass-scale, standing, national-backbone collection or network intelligence. The line is scale plus standing infrastructure, never the underlying technique.

Wildcard tier and the sovereig...

Wildcard tier and the sovereign ceiling

One vendor is graded at the edge of the rubric with explicit caution, and above all vendors sits the force that is not a vendor at all.

  • Allot — the Wildcard, and its status is the ambiguity. It markets "Actionable Network Intelligence"22 and has announced a government "national rollout" for a solution to "handle massive traffic load."23 But its dominant self-positioning is telecom Quality-of-Experience for carriers. The QoE-versus-intelligence straddle is the stated finding; no mass-surveillance capability is asserted.
  • The Tier-0 non-vendor force — the in-house build. The most capable buyers do not buy mass-collection infrastructure; they build it. The public anchor is XKEYSCORE, the NSA's documented "widest-reaching" system for Digital Network Intelligence.24 It sets the ceiling on what a vendor can sell and removes the most sophisticated buyers from the commercial market entirely — so the gradeable commercial universe is smaller than the demand.

The Cut Line: Who Is Not Graded, and Why

The front's apparent depth in popular discourse — surely Palantir, surely Cognyte — forces a defensible cut. Each name is placed where its public record puts it, never where internal knowledge might.

  • Cognyte is graded (Attention) — on its own network-intelligence/SIGINT glossary, and nothing else.   • Palantir — referenced, not graded. Its public government surface markets the decision-and-operational layer (Gotham, Maven)2526, not standing mass collection at a backbone. It is graded in Fusion on a Fusion-specific pillar.
  • Sandvine / AppLogic Networks — not graded on current positioning. It has publicly and deliberately moved toward telecom-QoE observability and away from the intelligence framing27; grading a mass-collection paragraph on an outdated pillar would misstate the record. Carried as the front's headline play instead.
  • The mobile-implant cohort (NSO / Candiru / Intellexa class) is out of scope for this front entirely — a distinct modality that does not collect at a network backbone.

1.4 Their Plays

1.4 Their Plays

Three strategic moves are in motion, and the count is held to three by the strictest evidence floor in the report: every play anchors to a dated public event or official record, or it is dropped, not softened.

  • Play 1 — The Encrypted-Traffic-Erosion Pivot. Passive collection is compressing as encryption hardens; the public record shows vendors pivoting from inspection to inference — ETI, behavioural fingerprinting, metadata and subscriber correlation.282930 The same encryption wave that compresses field-RF interception compresses national-backbone collection.
  • Play 2 — The Accountability & Sanctions Reset. The most-cited commercial DPI vendor was reset by the US export-control regime, on the record, inside roughly a year: a single Entity-List designation attached to ~45% of its disclosed revenue base31, then a cured removal, restructuring, and rebrand.32 A market-structure event, not a casualty judgment.
  • Play 3 — The Build-vs-Buy Ceiling. The most capable demand is not addressable by any vendor because the most sophisticated buyers build. The commercial market is the addressable remainder — mid-tier agencies without a sovereign build, plus the OEM engine layer even some sovereign programs draw on.

1.5 War Chests & Casualties

1.5 War Chests & Casualties

This front's capital structure is a public-issuer head and an opaque-specialist tail, and the verdict follows the same shape. Three pattern claims carry the front, sharing one throughline: transport encryption is hollowing out the classic value proposition of mass passive collection. The value it sheds migrates up the stack toward metadata and inference, and toward the accountable end of the supply side, where sanctions and export control now gate who may sell. Every figure traces to a named outlet, an official record, or the vendor's own dated filing; live market caps are governed by the disclaimer on the publication date, never fixed here.

  • Only Allot and BAE disclose, and both are growing. Allot returned to GAAP profit on ~$102M FY2025 revenue with SECaaS ARR up 69%.11 BAE's Cyber & Intelligence segment reports £2.4B inside record group sales33; neither breaks out a standalone SIGINT line. Below the public tier, capital goes dark: ipoque's numbers vanish inside the privately held Rohde & Schwarz group (€3.16B)34, and the Indian and German specialists (Vehere, ClearTrail, Trovicor, Kommlabs) raise no disclosed venture capital and publish no valuations.35363738

1.6 Winning & Losing

1.6 Winning & Losing

  • Winners — BAE Systems and ipoque. Both rest on disclosed evidence, not asserted capability. BAE wins the prime-scale multi-source-intelligence posture; ipoque wins the engine that decides whether the front can still classify anything as encryption rises. This is winning the reframe, not a share war.
  • Watch — Allot and AppLogic Networks. Allot's placement is the QoE-versus-intelligence straddle itself, not its balance sheet. AppLogic is the cured-and-repositioned subject of the accountability reset — resolved as neither winner nor loser because it walked out of the intelligence framing. The category's one public capital-and-regulatory shock — Sandvine's 2024 listing, same-year removal, and rebrand — is a cured event, not an open casualty.
  • Losers — none graded. No contender's balance sheet is implicated; the public events are growth and a cured regulatory episode. Quarterly refreshes will populate this line only if a collection-specific distress signal emerges on the public record.

1.7 The Campaign Ahead

1.7 The Campaign Ahead

Five watchlist items for H2 2026. Each names a public, observable threshold that would confirm or refute a claim above.

  • A named encrypted-traffic-intelligence government or carrier reference — the erosion pivot made concrete, versus a demonstrated durable content-visibility break the other way.
  • A further Entity-List action or a sanctions repositioning — whether the accountability reset is a durable, supply-concentrating force or a transient episode.
  • A named sovereign-build-versus-commercial procurement outcome — the build-vs-buy ceiling tested at the seam.
  • Allot's intelligence-versus-QoE disclosure mix — which market the Wildcard is actually in.
  • A named 5G-standalone collection-architecture reference — whether the 5G-SA refresh cycle is pulling standing-collection procurement forward.
